Couples therapy in India still carries stigma — the idea that seeking relationship counselling means your relationship is failing, or that airing your partnership's difficulties to a stranger is inappropriate.

## What Is Couples Therapy?

Couples therapy (also called relationship counselling or couples counselling) is a structured intervention where two partners work with a licensed therapist to improve their relationship.

**What couples therapy can address:**
- Communication breakdowns — talking past each other, escalation patterns, stonewalling
- Trust and infidelity — rebuilding after betrayal
- Sexual and intimacy difficulties
- Parenting disagreements and co-parenting conflict
- In-law tensions and extended family pressure
- Financial conflict
- Life transitions — moving in together, marriage, having children, relocation
- Relationship anxiety and attachment insecurity
- Unresolved individual issues affecting the relationship
- Considering separation — clarifying the decision with support

**What couples therapy cannot fix:**
- One partner who is not genuine about change
- Active addiction without concurrent individual addiction treatment
- Ongoing domestic abuse — individual safety plans are required before couples work

## Does Couples Therapy Actually Work?

Yes — with evidence comparable to individual therapy.

The Gottman Institute's decades of research shows that **80–90% of couples report improved relationship satisfaction following Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT)**, the most evidence-backed couples modality. A 2020 meta-analysis in *Journal of Marital and Family Therapy* confirmed that couples therapy significantly outperforms control conditions for communication, intimacy, and relationship satisfaction.

The research also shows something important: **the earlier couples seek help, the better the outcomes**. On average, couples wait 6 years after problems develop before seeking therapy. By that point, negative interaction patterns are deeply entrenched and harder to shift.

## The Main Evidence-Based Approaches to Couples Therapy

### Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT)

The gold standard for couples work, developed by Dr Sue Johnson. EFT is based on adult attachment theory — the idea that relationship distress is fundamentally about attachment insecurity (feeling unsafe, unseen, or disconnected from your partner).

EFT identifies and shifts the negative interaction cycles ("protest polka" — pursue/withdraw, demand/defend) that maintain disconnection. It helps partners access and express the vulnerable emotions beneath their defensive reactions.

**Evidence:** 70–75% of couples move from distress to recovery; 90% show significant improvement. Effects are maintained at 2-year follow-up.

### Gottman Method Couples Therapy

Based on Dr John Gottman's 40 years of research into what distinguishes couples who thrive from those who don't. Identifies the "Four Horsemen" of relationship apocalypse: criticism, contempt, defensiveness, and stonewalling — and teaches specific antidotes.

Particularly effective for: communication problems, conflict resolution, and couples who respond well to structured techniques.

### Cognitive Behavioural Couples Therapy (CBCT)

Applies CBT principles to the relationship: identifying and challenging negative attributions partners make about each other's behaviour, building communication skills, and modifying problematic interaction patterns.

Effective for: relationships where cognitive patterns (mind-reading, catastrophising, negative attribution) are a primary problem.

## Online Couples Therapy in India: What to Expect

Online couples therapy works similarly to in-person — both partners on the same video call with the therapist, or occasionally in separate locations. Research shows online couples therapy produces equivalent outcomes to in-person for most issues.

**Practical considerations:**
- You need a private space where both partners can speak freely without being overheard
- A single device works (both partners on screen together) or two devices (if in different locations)
- Sessions are typically 60–90 minutes (longer than individual therapy due to the dynamics involved)
- Weekly sessions are standard for active work; some couples move to biweekly once patterns stabilise

**What to look for in an online couples therapist in India:**
- Specific training in couples therapy or relationship counselling (not just individual therapy)
- Familiarity with EFT, Gottman, or CBCT approaches
- Experience with Indian relationship contexts: joint family dynamics, arranged marriage dynamics, cultural expectations
- Non-judgmental stance on diverse relationship structures

## Couples Therapy and the Indian Cultural Context

Couples therapy in India has specific cultural dimensions that a good therapist will understand:

**Joint family dynamics:** Conflicts with in-laws, expectations of the extended family, and pressure from parents about the relationship are significant sources of relationship stress in Indian contexts. A therapist who understands these dynamics will not apply Western-individualist frameworks that are irrelevant or unhelpful.

**Arranged vs. love marriage:** The dynamics, expectations, and pressure points differ significantly between arranged and love marriages. A skilled Indian couples therapist understands both.

**Cultural guilt and obligation:** Many Indian relationship difficulties involve conflicts between personal needs and strong cultural obligations (to parents, to community, to caste/religious expectations). A therapist who pathologises these as simply "codependency" without cultural understanding is missing important context.

**Gender dynamics:** Traditional gender role expectations create specific patterns in Indian couples — who is expected to sacrifice, who is expected to provide, and who is permitted to express emotional needs. Good couples therapy navigates these explicitly.

## When Should You Start Couples Therapy?

Research consistently shows: earlier is better. Start when:

- The same argument keeps happening, with neither partner feeling heard
- Physical and emotional intimacy has declined significantly
- You're co-existing rather than connecting
- Communication feels impossible on specific topics
- One or both partners are considering whether to stay
- A specific event (infidelity, job loss, loss of a child) has created a rupture that hasn't healed
- You want to build a stronger foundation before a major life transition (marriage, having children, relocation)

You don't need to be at crisis point. Couples who start therapy when problems are emerging have much better outcomes than those who wait until they're at breaking point.
