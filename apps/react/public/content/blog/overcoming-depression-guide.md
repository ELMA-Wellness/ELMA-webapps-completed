## Depression Is Treatable — But You Need the Right Information

Over 56 million Indians live with depression, making it one of the country's most common health conditions. And yet 80% receive no treatment — not because treatment doesn't exist, but because most people don't know what works, what to expect, or how to access it.

This guide covers the full landscape: what depression actually is, which treatments have the strongest evidence, what you can do today, and how to access professional support in India without spending a fortune.

---

## What Depression Actually Is (And What It Isn't)

Depression is not sadness. Sadness is a normal emotion — it is proportionate, reactive, and resolves. Depression is a syndrome: a cluster of symptoms that persist for two weeks or more and significantly impair functioning.

**Clinical diagnostic criteria (DSM-5 — five or more for at least two weeks):**
- Depressed mood most of the day, nearly every day
- Markedly diminished interest or pleasure in all (or almost all) activities (anhedonia)
- Significant weight change or change in appetite
- Insomnia or hypersomnia
- Psychomotor agitation or retardation (observable by others)
- Fatigue or loss of energy nearly every day
- Feelings of worthlessness or excessive guilt
- Difficulty thinking, concentrating, or making decisions
- Recurrent thoughts of death, suicidal ideation, or suicide attempt

Depression exists on a spectrum. You can have symptoms without meeting the full clinical threshold. Sub-clinical depression — sometimes called persistent depressive disorder or dysthymia — involves lower-intensity symptoms that persist for two years or more.

**The key distinction:** depression is not a character flaw, a sign of weakness, or something you can "push through." It involves measurable changes in brain chemistry, neural circuitry, and hormonal regulation. Telling someone with depression to "just be positive" is like telling someone with a broken leg to walk it off.

## The Evidence-Based Treatments for Depression

### 1. Cognitive Behavioural Therapy (CBT) — Gold Standard for Mild to Moderate Depression

CBT is the most extensively researched psychological treatment for depression. A 2021 Cochrane Review of 91 randomised controlled trials confirmed that CBT significantly outperforms control conditions for moderate depression across populations.

**How it works:**
CBT targets the cognitive triad of depression: negative beliefs about the self ("I am worthless"), the world ("Nothing works out"), and the future ("Nothing will ever improve"). These beliefs are learned — and they can be unlearned through structured examination.

Core CBT techniques:
- **Thought records:** Identifying automatic negative thoughts, examining the evidence for and against them, generating balanced alternatives
- **Behavioural activation:** Scheduling activities that provide pleasure or mastery, even when motivation is absent
- **Behavioural experiments:** Testing the accuracy of depressive predictions ("If I go to this event, nothing good will happen") against actual outcomes
- **Activity monitoring:** Tracking how activities affect mood to identify what actually helps vs. what the depression predicts will help

**Timeline:** Most people see measurable improvement in 6–16 sessions of weekly CBT. Some people need longer.

### 2. Antidepressant Medication — For Moderate to Severe Depression

For moderate to severe depression, antidepressants significantly increase recovery rates. The combination of medication and therapy outperforms either alone.

**First-line medications in India** (prescribed by a psychiatrist):
- SSRIs (selective serotonin reuptake inhibitors): Fluoxetine, Sertraline, Escitalopram
- SNRIs (serotonin-norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors): Venlafaxine, Duloxetine

**Important caveats:**
- Antidepressants are not the right choice for everyone — a psychiatrist assessment is required
- They take 4–6 weeks to reach therapeutic effect
- Stopping abruptly causes withdrawal symptoms — always taper under medical guidance
- They are a biological intervention for a biological component, not a "happy pill"

If you think you may need medication, please see a psychiatrist (not a GP for initial assessment). In India, Practo Consult provides telepsychiatry. NIMHANS has outpatient services.

### 3. Behavioural Activation — For Mild Depression (Effective Standalone)

A 2021 Cochrane Review found Behavioural Activation (BA) was as effective as full CBT for mild to moderate depression — with a simpler protocol that's easier to self-administer.

The core principle: depression creates inactivity, and inactivity deepens depression. BA breaks this cycle by scheduling activities based on a plan, not based on how you feel.

**A 4-week self-guided BA protocol:**

**Week 1:** Activity monitoring only. Record what you do each hour and rate your mood (0–10) before and after each activity. No changes yet — just observe.

**Week 2:** Analyse the data. Which activities consistently improve your mood, even slightly? Which consistently worsen it?

**Week 3:** Increase the activities that improve mood. Schedule them as calendar events, not intentions. Start small — 15-minute walks count.

**Week 4:** Address avoidance. Depression causes avoidance of meaningful activities that feel "too hard." Identify one avoided activity per week and schedule it, accepting that it will feel effortful.

### 4. Exercise — Comparable to Antidepressants for Mild to Moderate Depression

A 2024 meta-analysis in *The BMJ* covering 218 RCTs found that exercise was as effective as antidepressants for mild to moderate depression — with no side effects and additional physical health benefits.

**The effective dose:** 30 minutes of moderate-intensity aerobic exercise, three times per week. The specific activity matters less than consistency: walking, cycling, swimming, dancing, team sports — all qualify.

**Why exercise works:** It increases BDNF (brain-derived neurotrophic factor, which promotes neuroplasticity), reduces cortisol, improves sleep quality, and creates the experience of accomplishing something — which is psychologically powerful when depression has stripped your sense of efficacy.

The hardest part: starting when you have zero motivation. The solution is the smallest possible first step — put on shoes, go outside for 10 minutes. Momentum builds from tiny actions.

### 5. Interpersonal Therapy (IPT) — Especially Effective for Relationship-Related Depression

IPT focuses on the relationship between depression and interpersonal problems: grief, role disputes, life transitions, and social isolation. It has strong evidence for depression that is clearly connected to relationship difficulties or major life changes.

### 6. Sleep Intervention

Poor sleep and depression have a bidirectional relationship — each worsens the other. Treating insomnia alone significantly reduces depression symptoms.

**The most important sleep intervention for depression:**
- Fixed wake time every day (including weekends)
- No screens 60 minutes before bed
- No lying in bed awake — if not asleep in 20 minutes, get up
- Cool, dark room (18–20°C)

## Self-Help for Depression: What Works and What Doesn't

### What Actually Works

**Affect labelling (emotional naming):** Research by Lieberman at UCLA shows that naming emotions precisely reduces amygdala activation. Specific labels ("I feel hopeless and ashamed") are more effective than vague ones ("I feel bad").

**Behavioural activation** (see above): The most evidence-backed self-help technique for depression.

**Social contact:** Isolation is both a symptom and a driver of depression. Even one genuine conversation per day makes a measurable difference. Reciprocal contact (both parties sharing) is more effective than one-sided support.

**Morning light exposure:** 10+ minutes of natural light within 30 minutes of waking regulates the circadian rhythm disrupted by depression.

### What Doesn't Work Well as Sole Interventions

**Positive affirmations:** No strong clinical evidence for depression. Can backfire by creating a gap between felt experience and stated belief.

**Gratitude journalling:** Positive effect in general populations; limited evidence for active depressive episodes.

**Unguided journalling:** Mixed results — can increase rumination. Structured journalling with prompts is more effective.

## Getting Started: The Depression Recovery Sequence

This is the evidence-based sequence for starting depression recovery:

**Days 1–7:**
- Download ELMA (free) — start daily emotion tracking to build self-awareness
- Set a fixed wake time and stick to it regardless of how you feel
- Add one 15-minute walk outdoors per day

**Weeks 2–4:**
- Begin Behavioural Activation: track your activities and their effect on mood
- Identify your three highest-mood activities and schedule them daily
- If symptoms are moderate or severe, book a consultation with a professional

**Month 2 onward:**
- Continue BA + exercise as your foundation
- If working with a therapist, introduce CBT techniques

## When to Seek Professional Help Urgently

Please contact a professional immediately if you are experiencing:
- Thoughts of suicide or self-harm
- Inability to care for yourself (not eating, not sleeping, not functioning)
- Depression following childbirth (postpartum depression)
- Symptoms that have not improved after 4 weeks of self-help effort
