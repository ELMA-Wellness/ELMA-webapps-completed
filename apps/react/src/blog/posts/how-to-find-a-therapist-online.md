## The Hardest Part of Therapy Is Starting

Most people spend months — sometimes years — knowing they should find a therapist before they actually do. The barrier isn't money or availability. It's the overwhelming uncertainty of not knowing where to start, what to say, or whether the person you pick will be right for you.

This guide removes that uncertainty. By the end, you'll know exactly how to find a therapist online, what credentials to verify, what questions to ask, and how to handle the case where the first therapist isn't the right fit.

---

## Step 1: Get Clear on What You're Looking For

Before you search for anything, spend 10 minutes answering these questions honestly:

**What's bringing you to therapy right now?**
- Anxiety, panic attacks, or chronic worry
- Low mood, depression, or feeling flat and disconnected
- Relationship difficulties (romantic, family, friendships)
- Work stress, burnout, or career-related anxiety
- Grief, loss, or a major life transition
- Trauma or past experiences affecting your present
- Self-esteem, people-pleasing, or difficulty with boundaries
- General personal growth — you want to understand yourself better

**What format works for your life?**
- Video call (face-to-face feeling, from home)
- Voice call (lower barrier, no camera required)
- Text chat (asynch, lower immediate pressure)
- In-person (if your city has availability)

**What is your practical budget?**
In India, online therapy ranges from ₹700 to ₹5,000 per session depending on the therapist's experience. Knowing your budget upfront prevents the disappointment of connecting with someone you can't afford.

## Step 2: Understand the Credential Landscape in India

This is where most people get confused — and where some platforms cut corners.

In India, the mental health credential landscape is:

| Qualification | What It Means | Scope |
|---|---|---|
| MBBS + MD Psychiatry | Psychiatrist | Medical diagnosis, medication, therapy |
| M.Phil. Clinical Psychology (RCI) | Clinical Psychologist | Full clinical assessment and treatment |
| M.A./M.Sc. Psychology | Counselling Psychologist | Counselling, CBT, supportive therapy |
| RCI-registered professional | Rehab Council of India regulated | Legally regulated scope |
| Certified CBT/REBT therapist | Technique certification | Focused CBT/REBT sessions only |

**Red flag:** Anyone marketing themselves as a "therapist" or "counsellor" without specifying their qualification. In India, these terms are not legally protected — anyone can use them.

**What you need for common issues (anxiety, stress, burnout, relationships):** An M.A./M.Sc. Psychology minimum, with relevant experience.

**What you need for clinical conditions (depression disorder, PTSD, OCD, eating disorders):** RCI-registered clinical psychologist or psychiatrist (for assessment/medication).

## Step 4: How to Evaluate a Therapist Before Booking

Once you have 2–3 options, evaluate each on these dimensions.

### Check Their Profile For:

- Specific experience with your issue (not just "general counselling")
- Year of graduation and post-graduation training
- Any specialisations: CBT, trauma-informed, DBT, psychodynamic, EMDR
- Client reviews (where available — not all platforms show these)
- Whether they offer an initial consultation or intake call

### Questions to Ask Before Your First Session:

1. "What approach do you use, and why do you think it fits my situation?"
2. "Have you worked with clients dealing with [your issue] before?"
3. "How do you measure progress in therapy? When would we expect to see results?"
4. "What happens if I feel we're not a good match?"
5. "What is your policy if I need to cancel or reschedule?"

A good therapist welcomes these questions. Anyone who seems defensive about them is a yellow flag.

## Step 5: Your First Session — What to Expect

The first session is almost always an assessment/intake session, not a deep dive. Knowing this reduces the pressure of "performing" in it.

**Typical structure (45–60 minutes):**
- 0–10 min: Logistics, confidentiality explanation, informed consent
- 10–30 min: Your story — what's bringing you to therapy now
- 30–45 min: Background — family, work, health, relationships, relevant history
- 45–55 min: Goal-setting — what would better look like?
- 55–60 min: Next steps — frequency, approach, any initial homework

**You don't need to:**
- Have your issues perfectly articulated
- Know your diagnosis
- Have hit a crisis point — you can start therapy because you want to grow

**Bring:**
- Honesty about what's actually going on (this session determines the entire treatment direction)
- Specific examples of when things feel most difficult
- Your questions from Step 4

## Step 6: How to Know If Your Therapist Is the Right Fit

Therapeutic fit is one of the strongest predictors of outcome — stronger, research suggests, than the specific modality used.

After 2–3 sessions, ask yourself:

**Green flags:**
- You feel heard, not judged
- They challenge you in ways that feel useful, not dismissive
- The session structure feels purposeful, not just venting
- You leave with something concrete — a framework, a question to sit with, a technique to try
- You feel slightly uncomfortable but understood — growth lives in that discomfort

**Yellow flags:**
- Every session feels the same with no clear direction
- They talk more about their own experiences than your situation
- You feel you need to manage their reactions
- No measurable change after 8+ sessions

**What to do if it's not working:**
You can change therapists. This is not failure — it's information. Tell your therapist you want to try a different approach or a different person. Most platforms support this. A good therapist will help you transition rather than take it personally.
