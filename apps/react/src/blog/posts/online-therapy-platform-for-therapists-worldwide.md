## The Global Landscape of Online Therapy Platforms

Online therapy platforms fall into five distinct categories. Understanding which category a platform belongs to determines whether it fits your goals.

### Category 1: Marketplace Platforms (Volume-Based)

These platforms connect large numbers of clients with therapists at standardised rates. The platform handles marketing and client acquisition; you handle sessions.

**Examples:** BetterHelp (US/Global), Talkspace (US), YourDOST (India)

**Income model:** Platform sets the rate; you receive a percentage (typically 50–75%)

**Pros:** High client volume, no acquisition effort, administrative simplicity

**Cons:** Lower per-session rates, limited clinical autonomy, platform owns the client relationship, often subscription-model which clients cancel

**Best for:** Therapists who are new to online work and want a consistent caseload without marketing effort

### Category 2: Curated / Vetted Networks

These platforms have selective intake processes and often serve corporate EAP (Employee Assistance Programme) clients alongside direct consumers.

**Examples:** 1to1help (India EAP), Spring Health (US corporate), Lyra Health (US corporate)

**Income model:** Set corporate rates, often higher than consumer marketplace rates

**Pros:** High-quality clients with stable employment, more reliable session completion rates

**Cons:** Can be volume-constrained, long onboarding processes, restrictive clinical protocols

**Best for:** Experienced therapists with strong credential profiles who want corporate clients

### Category 3: AI-First Platforms (The Emerging Category)

These platforms combine an AI companion layer with human therapist sessions. The AI handles daily emotional support, pattern tracking, and triage; therapists handle structured sessions with clients who arrive pre-qualified and emotionally engaged.

**Examples:** ELMA Experts (India and global)

**Income model:** Session-based, with therapist retaining strong percentage; clients are pre-qualified by AI

**Pros:** Higher session quality, clients arrive having done emotional work, better retention, AI provides session context

**Cons:** Newer category, requires comfort with AI-integrated workflow

**Best for:** Therapists who want to work with motivated, self-aware clients and leverage data to make sessions more efficient

**[ELMA Experts](https://elma.ltd/en/elma-experts)** is the clearest current example of this model. Clients use the ELMA AI companion for daily emotional practice, mood tracking, and pattern awareness — then book sessions with ELMA Experts when they need structured professional support. Therapists receive clients who have already done significant self-reflection, reducing the "warm-up" time that makes early sessions inefficient.

### Category 4: Self-Managed Telehealth Practice

You handle your own client acquisition, scheduling, and administration — using a telehealth platform purely for the video infrastructure.

**Examples:** SimplePractice (US), Halaxy (Australia), Practo Consult (India, partially), Zoom Health (global)

**Income model:** You set and keep all your rates; platform takes a subscription fee, not a commission

**Pros:** Full clinical autonomy, highest per-session income, you own the client relationship

**Cons:** You handle all marketing, scheduling, billing, no-shows, and client acquisition

**Best for:** Experienced therapists with an existing referral network or content-based client acquisition (blogs, social media)

### Category 5: Hybrid (Platform + Private Practice Portfolio)

Most successful online therapists don't use a single model. They combine a platform (for consistent volume), a private caseload (for higher rates and autonomy), and sometimes a content or community-based channel (for passive client acquisition).

## Key Criteria for Evaluating Any Online Therapy Platform

### 1. Per-Session Income After Platform Commission

Calculate what you actually take home per session, not the headline rate.

| Platform Type | Headline Rate | Platform Take | Therapist Net |
|---|---|---|---|
| Large marketplace (e.g. BetterHelp) | $90–120/session | 30–50% | $45–70/session |
| EAP network | $75–100/session | Fixed | $75–100 minus overhead |
| AI-first platform (ELMA Experts) | INR/session (varies) | Reasonable % | Session-rate based |
| Self-managed (SimplePractice) | You set rate | ~$80/month flat | Full session rate |

Always ask for the actual payment structure before onboarding.

### 2. Client Quality and No-Show Rates

High volume means nothing if clients don't attend or disengage after two sessions. Ask platforms:
- What is the average session completion rate per client?
- What is the average client tenure (how many sessions does a typical client complete)?
- How are no-shows handled — do you get paid?

Clients who arrive pre-engaged with their own emotional work (as in AI-first platforms) have significantly lower no-show and dropout rates.

### 3. Clinical Autonomy

Some platforms dictate session structure, diagnostic approaches, or require specific documentation formats. If you have a specialised modality or clinical approach, ensure the platform accommodates it.

### 4. Onboarding Timeline and Requirements

Platforms that are selective (as they should be) require credential verification. Know the full onboarding process:
- Credential verification timeline (days to weeks)
- Reference requirements
- Supervision requirements (some require current supervision)
- Platform-specific training (some require proprietary method training)

ELMA Experts requires M.A./M.Sc. Psychology minimum and takes approximately 5 minutes to apply — with credential verification as a next step.

### 5. Payment Terms and Currency

For international therapists:
- What currencies does the platform pay in?
- Are there international transfer fees?
- How frequent are payments (weekly, monthly)?
- Is income in INR, USD, GBP, or another currency?

### 6. Technical Infrastructure

- Video platform quality (Zoom integration, proprietary platform, latency)
- Session recording policies (required for some insurance purposes)
- Data security and HIPAA/GDPR compliance
- Client communication tools (in-platform messaging vs. external)

## Why AI-First Platforms Are the Future of Therapy Practice

The ELMA model represents a fundamental shift in how therapy practices work — and understanding it helps you evaluate whether this model fits your professional goals.

**The traditional therapy model problem:** A typical first therapy session spends 40–50% of its time on information gathering — what's brought the client in, their history, current symptoms. This is necessary but not therapeutically productive. Clients drop out after 2–3 sessions partly because the early sessions feel administrative rather than helpful.

**The AI-first solution:** When clients use ELMA for 2–4 weeks before booking their first Expert session, the therapist receives:
- A Mood Curve showing emotional patterns over the past weeks
- Emotion Flower Wheel data showing which emotional states the client most frequently experiences
- Diary summaries (client-permission-gated) providing context
- A client who has already done significant self-reflection

The result: first sessions are immediately therapeutic rather than administrative. Client retention improves because early sessions feel valuable. Therapists can do deeper work in fewer sessions.

This is not AI replacing therapists. It is AI handling the daily support layer and data collection so therapists can focus entirely on the high-value clinical work that only humans can do.
